Diese Seiten sind technisch und auf Englisch. Sie sammeln, was die Mitwirkenden von OpenAmpere über die Geräte herausgefunden haben, jeweils mit Quelle und wie sicher die Angabe ist. Device knowledge

What OpenAmpere knows about the devices it talks to: registers, behaviour, differences between types and firmware, and how OpenAmpere treats them. Every fact says how sure it is and where it comes from, so nobody has to search through hundreds of issues again. The website shows these pages too, linked from the app (Device knowledge, #512), built from main, so they can describe behaviour that is not released yet. Keep links relative; the build reports a link to a file or heading that does not exist.

Not official manufacturer information. These pages are compiled by the OpenAmpere contributors from public manufacturer documents, community projects and reports of owners. They can be incomplete or wrong. Changing settings of an inverter can affect its warranty and the rules of the grid connection. OpenAmpere has no affiliation with the manufacturers named here or with EKD; names are used only to describe compatibility. Nothing here is legal advice.

Status of a fact

StatusMeaning
documentedIn a document of the manufacturer (protocol, manual, datasheet).
derivedFrom another project or the community, not checked on a device by us.
confirmedSeen on a real device: a diagnostics report, an owner's test or observation.
openUnverified, an assumption, contradicted or still to check. Says what is missing.

A fact can move up when new evidence arrives; then its source gets the new evidence added, the old one stays. Decisions of OpenAmpere (how it treats a device, and why) are not facts: they are listed with a link to the issue or pull request that made them.

Sources

  • Allowed: public manufacturer documents (named, not copied into the repository), projects with a compatible license (credited in NOTICE), diagnostics reports and tests in our issues, and owners' reports.
  • Not public, but handed to owners (e.g. a protocol the manufacturer's support sends on request, marked "all rights reserved"): only the facts (register addresses, meanings, units), in own words. The document is named, but never quoted, never added to the repository and never passed on.
  • Not allowed: documents under NDA or meant only for partners, anything from the Ampere.IQ app or its decompiled code, projects whose license does not allow reuse.
  • In own words: register addresses and their meaning are facts; text, tables and the structure of a manufacturer document are not copied. Only the registers OpenAmpere needs, in our own layout.
  • How to cite: an issue or pull request of OpenAmpere as a link, e.g. #334; another project as owner/repo (with owner/repo#N for its issues) and, where it matters, the commit; a document by its title and date.
  • Private sources (e.g. a message or screenshot an owner sent the maintainer) are described, never linked: "an owner's report, 2026-10". Ask the owner whether the information may be used, without their name.

Privacy

Nothing that identifies a person or an installation: no names, addresses, serial numbers (also shortened ones), host names, exact system size (kWp, kWh) or rated power of an owner's system, the exact model and firmware of an owner's device, energy figures, dates and times of an owner's actions or outages. Settings of an owner's device are not quoted either; "matched the manufacturer's app" is enough. Round example values ("about 10 kW", "a FoxESS H3") are fine.

Keeping it current

New knowledge about a device goes onto its page in the same pull request as the code or the issue that brought it: a new register, a behaviour seen on a device, a confirmation, a correction. Add the status and the source; mark facts that turned out wrong as such instead of deleting them silently.

The short status (supported, confirmed, planned) of every device, and the list of what EKD sold, is kept in support.json (#526): the READMEs, devices.md (the table "Confirmed on a real system"), topics/names.md and the website are generated from it. When a confirmation on a device page changes that status, change it there too and run node scripts/device-support.mjs.
