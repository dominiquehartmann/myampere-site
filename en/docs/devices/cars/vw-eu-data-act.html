Volkswagen group EU Data Act portal (cars)

Not official information. Compiled by the OpenAmpere contributors; can be incomplete or wrong. No affiliation with the Volkswagen group or its brands, not legal advice. See Device knowledge.

Applies to: cars of Volkswagen, Audi, Škoda, SEAT and CUPRA with an account at the brand, through the group's EU Data Act portal eu-data-act.drivesomethinggreater.com · Sold as: – · OpenAmpere: car source vw_euda, src/openampere/cars/vw_euda.py · Support: reading state of charge, range, odometer, plug and charging state (read-only) · Confirmed on a real device: no (built from evcc's client; not yet tested against the real portal by the maintainers, #401)

Summary

  • Since June 2026 the brands' app interfaces only answer the manufacturer's own apps. The EU Data Act portal is the remaining way for owners to get their car's data without a paid service.
  • It is not a live interface: about every 15 minutes the portal stores a dataset (a zip archive with one JSON document of data points), some 15 minutes after the values were taken. Only reading, no control, no position.
  • The owner has to do one step in a browser first: log in, give consent, link the car and start a continuous data request. Without it the portal delivers nothing; the first data can take hours.
  • The login is the web login of the brand account. There is no token that lasts, so the password is kept.
  • The same portal and the same endpoints serve all five brands; only the identity client of the login differs.

Decisions in OpenAmpere

DecisionWhyIssue/PR
Read the portal at all, with the owner's login, as an optional, read-only source.The owner's own data through the channel the EU Data Act gives them; an AC wallbox cannot know the state of charge. Decided as an exception to "cloud-free".#401
Keep the password encrypted with secret.key (a backup holds it encrypted, unreadable without secret.key, which OpenAmpere's own backups do not contain; a Home Assistant backup of the app holds both, as for every stored secret), never show it; the VIN only as its last four characters in the app and not in reports or logs.The portal has no lasting token; the VIN identifies the car and its owner.#401
Follow login redirects only between the identity service and the portal, and accept addresses built from the login page (form action, password step) only on the identity service; never fetch the user page or the marketing page, give no consent.Credentials and cookies go nowhere else; consent is the owner's decision.#401
Look again a minute after the next dataset is due (its time + 16 minutes), at least 2 minutes apart; every 10 minutes when it is overdue, every 30 minutes before the first one, hourly while the owner still has a step to do in the portal. Download only datasets newer than the last one, at most a day at once; eight at the first look.Goes easy on the portal while new values arrive soon after they are there.#401
After the portal refused the login, do not retry by itself until the login is changed or the owner asks for the data.Repeated failed logins could lock the owner's account.#401
Store every delivery in OpenAmpere's own database and take the state of charge at plug-in and the odometer of a charge from it when they are shown: the last value up to 12 hours before the plug-in, otherwise the first up to 45 minutes after it; at the end the last value up to the unplugging, otherwise the first after it.The portal delivers late and keeps data only for a while; a parked car keeps its state of charge.#401
Keep the deliveries 90 days (about 100 rows a day); an ended charge keeps its state of charge at plug-in and at the end and its odometer (settled a day after it ended, when late deliveries are in) until the car is removed; the data of cars no longer set up is deleted; never log the car's name, login or VIN (the log is part of the diagnostics report), and pseudonymise them in reports through privacy.py.The analysis takes the state of charge at plug-in and the odometer from them; the log is shared in issues.#401
A charge is a plug-in at the wallbox OpenAmpere controls and that is linked to the car; its energy is what the wallbox counts. The wallbox control does not use the car's values.Only reading for now; control by state of charge would be its own, safety-relevant issue.#401
Send an honest user agent that names OpenAmpere.As for the grid meter portal.#174
Take only facts from other projects, no code.License and own words; credited in NOTICE.#401

Open questions

  • Nothing above was checked against the real portal by the maintainers. A report from an owner with a car of the group is needed: does the login work as described, which data points does their model deliver, how late are they?
  • Whether the portal turns away clients that do not look like a browser (the honest user agent).
  • Whether a car charged somewhere else should be told apart from a guest car at the same wallbox (today every plug-in at the linked wallbox counts as this car).
