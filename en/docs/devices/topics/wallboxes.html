Device knowledge

These pages are technical. They collect what the OpenAmpere contributors have found out about the devices, each fact with its source and how sure it is. They show the latest state of development, which can be ahead of the version you have installed. They are not official manufacturer information. You don't need them to set up OpenAmpere; the app explains everything you need.

Wallboxes through evcc, OCPP and directly

Not official manufacturer information. Compiled by the OpenAmpere contributors; can be incomplete or wrong. No affiliation with evcc or the manufacturers named here, not legal advice. See Device knowledge.

Applies to: wallboxes and vehicles supported by evcc; wallboxes that speak OCPP 1.6J (read-only check) · Sold as: – · OpenAmpere: src/openampere/evcc.py (evcc's REST API), src/openampere/ocpp.py (OCPP listener for the diagnostics) · Support: control through evcc; OCPP read-only · Confirmed on a real device: no

Summary

  • Most wallboxes are controlled through evcc. OpenAmpere gives evcc the site meters (grid, PV, battery), so evcc needs no Modbus connection of its own to the inverter.
  • A few wallboxes OpenAmpere reads itself, without evcc: it controls the portable dé wallbox over Tuya LAN, see tuya/de-portable.md, and only shows the FoxESS AC EV Charger over Modbus TCP, never writing to it (foxess/ac-charger.md, #439); its charging modes only simulate the control and log what OpenAmpere would set (#440). It also only shows the Weidmüller AC SMART ("AMPERE.Charge") over Modbus TCP (weidmueller/ac-smart.md, #523), and the go-e Charger over its local HTTP API (goe/charger.md, #597). Several of them, and an evcc wallbox next to them, share the place "wallbox" in the order of the solar surplus; one OpenAmpere only shows counts in the house load but is never acted on.
  • Which wallboxes work is decided by evcc. Many need a paid evcc sponsor token; the public trial token runs out after some days, and evcc then no longer starts the wallbox.
  • In evcc a loadpoint with the heating feature is a heat pump or heating rod; its "SoC" values are temperatures.
  • For a quick check, a wallbox can be pointed at OpenAmpere as an OCPP 1.6J central system for at most 15 minutes. OpenAmpere only asks questions and never starts, stops or limits charging.
  • The FoxESS AC EV Charger has its own page: foxess/ac-charger.md.

Decisions in OpenAmpere

DecisionWhyIssue/PR
Wallboxes OpenAmpere does not know itself are controlled through evcc's REST API (until #386 the only way); OpenAmpere serves evcc the site meters at /api/evcc/site.evcc supports many wallboxes; the inverter keeps a free connection slot.docs/devices.md, evcc.md
Wallboxes OpenAmpere knows itself are controlled without evcc (wallbox/native.py), so far the portable dé wallbox; evcc stays the way for all others.No sponsor token, no second program for a single wallbox.#318, #386, tuya/de-portable.md
wallbox/native.py has one model per wallbox with the same reading for all (base.Reading); a model can be read only (no write path at all): so far the FoxESS AC EV Charger. Its power is shown and counted in the house load, but the surplus control never acts on it; its modes other than "manual" only simulate the control, logged as not sent and never taking surplus from other devices (#440).Show a wallbox and how its control would behave before its write registers and watchdog are confirmed, without any risk.#439, #440, foxess/ac-charger.md
The sponsor token's state is shown in the app; a missing or expired token gets its own message; a date in the past counts as expired.An expired token stops the wallbox.#317, #319, #462
The limit of a heating loadpoint is a temperature capped at 20–90 °C; a car loadpoint uses 20–100 %.evcc uses the same field for both.#245, #249
evcc's battery priority (priority_soc) is sent only with control switched on and outside test mode, and it is logged.Same rules as every other control.#219, #226
OCPP listener for the diagnostics only: OCPP 1.6J, security profile 0, open for at most 15 minutes, at most 500 messages of 64 KiB per connection. BootNotification is answered with "Pending" (heartbeat 60 s), Heartbeat with the time; StatusNotification and MeterValues are recorded for the diagnostics report; everything else (Authorize, StartTransaction, …) gets CALLERROR NotSupported. OpenAmpere sends only GetConfiguration and GetCompositeSchedule; never start or stop, charging profiles or configuration changes.A read-only check of the wallbox without taking over its control.#328, #333
The OCPP listener refuses a WebSocket that carries an Origin header or a Host that is not allowed (as the rest of the app); it takes its single connection before the first check that waits, and the texts a wallbox sends (vendor, model, firmware, status, …) appear in the report as inline code.A wallbox sends no Origin, a browser always does: otherwise any web page opened in the home network could take the connection and write into the report. Applies to the Home Assistant app as well, where the wallbox connects directly, not through the sidebar.security fix (GHSA-q2mp-55hm-346m)
The app warns before the OCPP check that the wallbox may charge without a limit while it points at OpenAmpere.No charging profile is sent.#328, #333

Open questions

  • No wallbox has been confirmed with OpenAmpere on a real system (#111, #173, #185).
  • The OCPP listener is tested with a fake client only, not with a real wallbox (#333).
  • How long the evcc trial token lasts: "a few days" in the code against about ten days in #317; evcc may change it.
