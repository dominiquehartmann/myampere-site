Device knowledge

These pages are technical. They collect what the OpenAmpere contributors have found out about the devices, each fact with its source and how sure it is. They show the latest state of development, which can be ahead of the version you have installed. They are not official manufacturer information. You don't need them to set up OpenAmpere; the app explains everything you need.

FoxESS AC EV Charger

Not official manufacturer information: compiled by the OpenAmpere contributors, possibly incomplete or wrong. Changing device settings can affect warranty and grid connection rules. No affiliation with FoxESS or EKD, not legal advice. See Device knowledge.

Applies to: FoxESS AC EV Charger (wallbox, single- and three-phase models) · Sold as: "Ampere.ChargePro" (22 kW) by EKD (amperesolar.de/chargepro, documented; see names.md); owners report it with EKD (Ampere.IQ) systems (#412) · OpenAmpere: wallbox module foxess_ac, read-only wallbox check in the setup and live view (wallbox/native.py, #439), its work mode written on request (#464), its charging modes as a simulation without writing (#440) until the owner switches on real control (#465) · Support: display (live view and check) and the work mode on request (#464); control of charging as a test once the owner switches it on per wallbox (#465, offered as such in the setup since #503) · Confirmed on a real device: partly (connection and the read registers on one three-phase charger of an EKD system, #431)

Summary

  • The charger has three communication modes, only one active at a time: default (FoxESS app and cloud, with PV linkage to the inverter), Modbus TCP and OCPP.
  • OpenAmpere reads it: in a one-off check of the setup, to confirm the register addresses (#325), and as a wallbox it shows live (#439). The one register it writes, on the owner's request, is the work mode 0x3000 (#464); its charging modes are a simulation that only logs what OpenAmpere would set (#440). Control comes only after owners' reports confirm the registers (#318).
  • Modbus TCP: registers are holding registers read with FC03 (0x4000–0x4003 are write-only, FC06), unit id 1, port 502 (1502 also seen); port 502, unit id 1 and FC03 confirmed on one charger (#431).
  • On EKD systems the previous smartbox blocks OpenAmpere's access to the charger while it is powered; with the box cut from power the check reads the charger (#431).
  • In Modbus TCP mode the charger's watchdog fails in the unsafe direction: it keeps charging when the controller stops writing.
  • The register addresses come from FoxESS's protocol document V1.6, which FoxESS support gives to owners and an owner posted in a public evcc discussion; it is marked "all rights reserved", so they are used as facts only. The write registers (work mode, current and power setpoints, start/stop) are documented there, not yet tested by OpenAmpere (#463).

Registers

Read by the check over one connection: 0x1000–0x1021, 0x3000–0x3006 and, separately, 0x3007–0x300B (missing on single-phase models). The raw words go into the report through OpenAmpere's masking (serial-like text shortened, private words removed). Nothing is written.

Read by the live view (#439) every 10 s over one short connection, closed after reading: 0x1000–0x1015 and 0x3000–0x3006, then 0x3007–0x300B (skipped when the charger refuses it as unknown addresses, as single-phase models may). The energy counters, the card number, the model code and the serial number (0x1016–0x1031) are not read at all. Only function code 3; nothing is written.

The registers marked confirmed gave plausible values in the check on one three-phase charger of an EKD system, idle with no car plugged in (#431). According to the document, read/write registers are written with function code 16 and write-only registers with function code 6. OpenAmpere has not written any of them yet; evcc's driver (since July 2026) writes them on real devices (evcc-io/evcc#31412). Values while charging and single-phase models are not confirmed yet.

Behaviour

FactStatusSource
Modbus TCP over LAN or WLAN; port 502 according to the protocol, 1502 seen in at least one integration; unit id 1 by default; registers are holding registers read with FC03; read/write registers are written with FC16, the write-only registers 0x4000–0x4003 with FC06.documentedFoxESS EV Charger Modbus TCP protocol V1.6; #325
The charger announces Modbus TCP over mDNS as EVC-<serial>._modbus._tcp.derived#325, #327, probe.py
The wallbox that came with EKD (Ampere.IQ) systems is this charger: owners' wallboxes announce themselves as EVC-<serial> and are found by OpenAmpere's search; owners know it as "the EKD wallbox".derivedowner reports, #412
With evcc's foxess-evc template one owner's charger closed the connection at once (connection reset by peer). Likely causes: not in the Modbus TCP mode, or another client holds the connection (on EKD systems probably the previous smartbox, see below).openowner report, #412
Register lists of other charger families (e.g. holding registers 100, 300, 400) do not apply; an evcc custom charger built from them only timed out.derivedowner report, #412
Three communication modes, set in the Fox Switch app, one at a time: default (FoxESS app/cloud with PV linkage), Modbus TCP, OCPP.documentedFox Switch app manual V1.0.1; #318
In default mode the charger can be wired over RS485 to a FoxESS inverter (H3, H3 Pro, H3 Smart, see h3.md) or a shared meter and charges from surplus by itself: Fast (full current), Economic (solar first, at least 6 A from the grid), Green (surplus only). FoxESS requires self-use mode, no export limit and the order house → car → battery (the home battery may discharge into the car).documentedFoxESS energy management user manual; #318
In default mode Modbus TCP is read-only according to the Fox Switch app manual (section 8.6).documentedFox Switch app manual V1.0.1; #318
Outside the "Modbus TCP" mode some firmware answers every read with exception 6 ("server device busy").derivedevcc discussions; #318, #325
In Modbus TCP mode the watchdog fails in the unsafe direction: when the controller stops writing, the charger does not stop; after about 60 s it charges at its stored default current or at full power. Only an explicit stop ends charging, and a power setpoint written after a stop can restart it. Cost risk only; the charger stays within its rated limits.derivedowner reports from other projects, #318
A controller starts, stops and sets the current or power (0x3001/0x3002, 0x4001) only in work mode 0 ("controlled"); in plug and charge the charger starts by itself, and locked allows nothing.derivedFoxESS EV Charger Modbus TCP protocol V1.6; #318
A setpoint (0x3001/0x3002) must be written again within the validity (0x3005); otherwise the charger treats the controller as gone and charges with the fallback.derivedowner reports in evcc-io/evcc#26218
A setpoint written after a stop (0x4001 = 2) starts charging again without a start command. A controller must not refresh a setpoint while it has stopped the charger.derivedowner reports in evcc-io/evcc#26218
A current setpoint takes effect only while charging; outside a session the charger resets it.derivedowner reports in evcc-io/evcc#26218
Some chargers suddenly report work mode 2 ("locked", status 8); the previous app then shows the wallbox as locked and cannot change it.derivedowner report (#464)
Current and power setpoints are held in RAM and lost on a power cut; configuration values are probably stored. Automatic 1/3-phase switching follows the power setpoint.derived#318
The previous smartbox of EKD (Ampere.IQ) systems is a Modbus TCP client of the charger: its settings page lists the charger as "Ampere.ChargePro Bridge", Modbus TCP, host EVC-<serial>.local, unit id 1, port 502, marked as not configurable. So the box competes with OpenAmpere and evcc for the charger's connection(s).derivedan owner's screenshot of the box's settings page, #427; #412
While the previous smartbox is powered, OpenAmpere cannot read the charger: the connection on port 502 is refused, or it is accepted but the answers carry foreign transaction ids and then time out. With the box cut from power the check reads all blocks at once on port 502, unit id 1, FC03; port 1502 refused. Whether the charger allows only one client or mishandles concurrent clients is open.confirmed (one device)owner's diagnostics report, #431
Some chargers accept a single Modbus client only: a charger read by evcc may refuse a second connection or drop evcc's for a moment. The real number of connections is unknown.openprobe.py, #318
The installer's binding of the charger in the FoxESS app expires after 6 hours; owners can bind it to their own FoxESS cloud account.deriveddesk research in #318
OCPP: 1.6J works; 2.0.1 rejects charging profiles (FoxESS support recommends 1.6J). No phase switching over OCPP. After a server restart the charger can take many minutes to reconnect. In OCPP mode the FoxESS app/cloud and evcc lose control; the charge point id is the serial number. See wallboxes.derived#318
Newer models have a hardware input for the grid operator's dimming signal (§ 14a EnWG).derived#318
The FoxESS H3 has no registers for an attached charger; its power is part of the house load.documentedFoxESS inverter Modbus definition; #318

The live view opens one short connection per poll (every 10 s; after a failure again after 15 s, 30 s, … up to 5 minutes) and closes it after reading. Values older than 35 s are not used. In the live view the status, not 0x1005, tells whether a car is plugged in (status 1–5 and 9). Status 9 counts as charging, 8 (locked) as unknown with the work mode "locked". The modes off, solar, min + solar and fast are offered as a simulation only: native.py decides as for the dé wallbox (surplus, margin, two checks in a row, minimum on and off time, raising at most every 30 s, the owner's waiting time) with the charger's own limits (0x1013, 0x1014), and logs every start and stop it would have made, marked as a simulation and not sent, whatever the master switch and the test mode say. Changes of the current are collected and logged at most every 5 minutes per wallbox as one entry (the current then and the lowest and highest current since the entry before); a collected change is logged before the next stop or the end of the plan, and nothing while nothing changes, so the simulation, which also runs with the master switch off, does not bury real changes in the control log (review of #443). Without the owner's yes to real control the model stays read only for charging: its Modbus connection for reading still cannot write, and a test fails on any write request (the work mode is written on request, row for #464 below; real control, row for #465 below). The plan is kept in memory, taken over from the charger when a car is plugged in, and dropped with the mode "manual" or a restart. A charge the charger started by itself counts as its own; letting go of a charge the simulation started logs the explicit stop real control would send. Phases follow the automatic 1/3-phase switching (0x300A): one phase below the lowest three-phase power (6 A × 3), three from there, switched at most once per the charger's interval (0x300B, 5 minutes if not told). The plan never counts as load or takes surplus from other devices; the charger's real power stays in the house load. Home Assistant gets no mode for it.Shows on real data how the control would behave (when it would set what, how often, compared with what the charger does by itself) before anything may be written. The phase rule is derived from the protocol (switching follows the power setpoint), not confirmed on a device; the 5 minutes used when the charger does not report 0x300B are an assumption of OpenAmpere (open). The heartbeat and the fallback current (0x3006) are not simulated: they only matter once OpenAmpere writes (row for #465 below).#440, #318

The work mode (0x3000) is the one register OpenAmpere writes to the charger: chosen by the owner in the app after a question, with function code 16, only with the master switch on (logged, not sent in test mode), read back and logged. A ModbusWallbox writes only the registers it was made with (writable); the poll and the check get none.A charger that went "locked" cannot be released with the previous app; a stored setting needs no watchdog.#464
Real control (#465) only per wallbox after the owner agreed in the app, as a test: the modes off, solar, min + solar and fast write the current (0x3001, FC16) and start or stop (0x4001, FC06), only in work mode "controlled". Before the first write after a (re)connection 0x3005 = 60 s and 0x3006 = 6 A, nothing else written unless both read back; real control is switched on and off only with the question in the app, never by saving the wallbox list; test mode during a charge OpenAmpere started stops it and lets go; while the car charges the current is written again every 25 s (not logged), never after a stop; the current written before start or stop; on a planned shutdown and when OpenAmpere lets go (also the mode "manual") a charge it started is stopped; a stop never waits for the fallback current to be set again. Under real control OpenAmpere refuses any work mode other than "controlled"; a change outside OpenAmpere during a charge it started makes it send a stop and let go. A charger that does not read back 0x3005/0x3006 is not controlled for 15 minutes, and a charge OpenAmpere started is stopped. A lower current the charger keeps while charging (a limit of its own, or perhaps a dimming) counts as its highest current for 15 minutes, until the mode changes or OpenAmpere lets go; then the mode's current is asked again. With automatic switching on (0x300A) a three-phase charger gets its phases through the power setpoint 0x3002 (#478): 4.1 kW for one phase, for three the power setpoint the owner had before OpenAmpere first wrote it (else 0x1011; a value of exactly 4.1 kW counts as a leftover of OpenAmpere's own cap), written with the current, refreshed with the heartbeat, never after a stop, at most once per 0x300B. A one-phase cap OpenAmpere set (stored before it is written, its end only after the write, so also known after a restart) is put back to the owner's value before every stop, when OpenAmpere lets go (also without a charge; with a car connected that does not charge, followed by a stop, so the setpoint cannot restart a paused charge) and when automatic switching is turned off (tried again at most every 15 minutes if the charger does not take it); while OpenAmpere controls, it decides the phases and a power set in the FoxESS app meanwhile is overwritten (the value from before the control is what it puts back); the cap is put back only while the charger still reads it (another value means the owner set one since, and the cap is forgotten); a power limit the owner set is never raised: with an owner's setpoint below 4.2 kW OpenAmpere does not switch phases at all, and the one-phase value never exceeds the owner's. Without automatic switching 0x3002 stays untouched.The watchdog fails unsafe, so the owner decides; the pitfalls reported by owners (#463).#318, #465
A restored backup keeps the running installation's consent per wallbox (real control off for a wallbox it does not have; a difference is noted in the control log entry of the restore) and its record of which charges OpenAmpere started and stopped (wallbox_owned, wallbox_stopped, like remote_command for the inverter). Open questions

  • A write by OpenAmpere (work mode 0x3000, #464) is not yet confirmed on a real device. Open with it: whether the status leaves 8 ("locked") as soon as the work mode is no longer 2 (the simulator assumes so), and which exception a charger outside the "Modbus TCP" mode sends for a write (the app's text assumes 1, 2, 3 or 6).

  • Conflict, default mode: the Fox Switch app manual says Modbus TCP is read-only in default mode; evcc reports that some firmware refuses every read with exception 6 outside the Modbus TCP mode. Only a real device can settle whether reads work in default mode (#318, #325).

  • Number of simultaneous Modbus connections: while the previous smartbox is powered OpenAmpere cannot read the charger, but whether it allows only one client or mishandles concurrent clients is open (#431).

  • Whether the previous smartbox only reads the charger or also controls it (#431).

  • Meaning of 0x1005, which read 1 while idle with no car plugged in; needs a reading with a car plugged in (#431).

  • Meaning of alarm bit 1 in 0x1015, set on one device while idle (#431).

  • Real watchdog time and fallback (default current or full power); whether a stop holds after the link is lost; which configuration values are stored.

  • Whether PV linkage still runs in Modbus TCP mode.

  • Possible conflicts with OpenAmpere (no owner reports yet): OpenAmpere's export limit contradicts FoxESS's precondition for PV linkage, and the charger might see OpenAmpere's grid charging of the battery as surplus (#318, #327).

  • OCPP: whether charging profiles survive a reboot, expiry handling, 0 A, reconnect time. A fail-safe idea (stored base profile of 0 A or 6 A plus short-lived higher limits renewed by OpenAmpere) is not confirmed on any charger; renewing stored profiles may wear the charger's flash, a 0 A base profile blocks charging while OpenAmpere is down, and OCPP 1.6 leaves expiry and reboot behaviour open.

  • Meaning of the read but uninterpreted words (0x1000, 0x1002, 0x1004, 0x1006–0x1007, 0x100F–0x1010, 0x3007–0x3009).

  • No write by OpenAmpere tested on a real device yet: work mode, setpoints, start and stop (#464, #465).

  • How the charger reacts to a start or a setpoint while it switches phases (status 9), and how long a switch takes. OpenAmpere counts status 9 as a running charge: no start is sent, the heartbeat goes on (#478).

  • Whether 0x3001/0x3002 can be written outside the work mode "controlled". If OpenAmpere's one-phase cap does not read back when it lets go, it keeps a note and tries again every 15 minutes, four times in all; then the control log asks the owner to set the power in the Fox Switch app (#478).

  • Whether the charger resets or refuses 0x3002 outside a charge. Under control OpenAmpere does not count a differing read-back there as failed and checks it again once the car charges; when it lets go, it does (#478).

  • Whether the charger keeps the power setpoint 0x3002 after a stop or when the car is unplugged. OpenAmpere writes its owner's power setpoint back with every stop of a charge it capped at one phase and when it lets go (#478).

  • Whether a stop (0x4001 = 2) has any effect once the work mode is no longer "controlled", and whether the watchdog (0x3005) still applies in plug and charge (#465).

  • Why a charger switches to "locked" by itself (#464).
